Autonomous Payload Delivery Rover

Electrical design and hardware integration for a four-person undergraduate engineering design project.

Prototype demo

See the rover in action.

The rover carries the tennis ball toward the aluminum receiving tray, stops alongside it, and actuates the release mechanism. The ball is visible in the tray at the end of the clip.

01 / Objective

A five-foot delivery task.

Our four-person team was tasked with developing an autonomous rover to transport a tennis ball five feet and deposit it into an aluminum receiving tray. The design combined a motorized chassis, embedded control electronics and a servo-actuated payload release mechanism.

My contributions: CAD assembly drawings, hardware prototyping, breadboarding, wiring, and integration of the electronic components into the mechanical structure.

02 / Design

Mechanical packaging meets embedded control.

The team's rotating trapdoor was inspired by a gumball-machine mechanism, with an added overhang to help retain the ball. Commercially available control electronics and motors were integrated into the chassis.

System overview

03 / Iteration

Making the hardware work together.

We ran into battery problems, wiring issues and poor-quality 3D prints. A lot of my time went into getting the electronics and mechanical assembly to work together.

Shortly before the final demonstration, the team added a photosensor to detect the aluminum tray at approximately 5 cm and trigger stopping.

04 / Outcome

Successful final demonstration.

Primary delivery objective fulfilled.

The rover successfully transported the tennis ball five feet and deposited it into the aluminum tray during our final demonstration.

This project taught me how much reliable wiring, power delivery and mechanical fit matter when building a working system. If I were to revisit the design, I would improve the power supply and test the stopping mechanism more systematically.
